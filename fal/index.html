how I work

routing

no single model carries a film. every shot wants something else: one needs a face to hold for twelve frames, one needs fast camera movement, one needs a specific texture. each shot goes to the model that does that one thing best.

finishing

generated footage comes out too clean. no lens breathing, no sensor noise, light that is too even. so: grain matching, optical imperfection, a grade that behaves like film. the model gives you correct. correct is not real.

one constraint

a client once required a single toolset. a licensing decision, not an aesthetic one. no routing. so I changed what I asked of the model and moved the fix into post: grade, grain, optical flaws, an edit that kept the weakest shots short. the delivery held.

when you can't change the model, you change what you ask of it. the constraint was legal, not technical. a better model doesn't fix that.

what breaks where

two years of testing, condensed: where these models still break.

  • the same face drifting across twelve shots
  • hands. still the weakest place.
  • motion falling apart when the camera speeds up
  • light that shifts direction and temperature between cuts
  • physics: fabric, water, hair
  • temporal flicker, backgrounds that boil
  • the opposite problem: footage so clean it reads as fake

version numbers rot. these patterns don't. a metric can't say the character's hand is wrong. an eye that has cut film sees it instantly.

what I'd build here

01

reference pipelines

every line I run today I built myself, from scratch, on every project: the shot breakdown, the routing, the retry logic, the finishing chain. that should be infrastructure, not private workarounds every director rebuilds alone.

02

model evaluation, with a director's eye

a benchmark tells you how fast a model renders. it doesn't tell you whether the light holds when the cut comes, or whether the motion blur feels organic. for that you need a film person in the loop.

both come back to the same sentence: the generation call is the easy part. everything around it is the work.
