01

Research

The building dates to 1205–06: a hospital and a medical school side by side, built by Sultan Gıyaseddin Keyhüsrev I after the will of his sister Gevher Nesibe. The hospital stands on the west, the Gıyasiye medical school on the east; both have an open courtyard with four iwans and a pool. The hospital had vaulted patient rooms, and no dome and no glazed tiles.

For the medicine of the time we followed the sources: diagnosis by pulse and by the glass flask, diet, cupping and remedies like theriac, taught at the bedside and then discussed in the great iwan. Anything the sources could not confirm stayed out of the film.

02

Script and voice

The script is built as a circle: the faculty of today and the hospital of 800 years ago answer each other, and the film ends where it started. The voice-over was recorded in Turkish, calm and documentary, not an advert voice.

“Eight centuries later, on the same ground, the same three aims.”

03

Storyboard

The whole film was planned on a 52-panel storyboard called “Bakış” (the gaze) before a single final frame was made. The first opening panels were rough placeholders; research later removed what did not belong in 1206, like the domes and the illustrated anatomy book seen here.

04

Blender model

We built the whole complex as a grey block model in Blender from the published survey plans, with 23 fixed cameras. The model never appears in the film. It holds the architecture: walls, arches and openings stay where they are in every shot.

The model also fixes the real orientation. The gate faces south, and Mount Erciyes stands about 22 km to the south, so the mountain only enters the frame from the north or from above. One deliberate change: the gate's door is wider than in the survey, so it reads on screen.

05

AI frames

Each frame was generated on top of a grey render. The model locks the architecture; AI adds light, stone, cloth and people. Every character came from a fixed design sheet, so the same faces return from shot to shot.

AI kept adding things from later centuries, so every frame was checked: no domes or tiles on the hospital, none of Kayseri's later monuments on the skyline, no illustrated anatomy books, no Asclepius staff, books stacked flat as they were. The city stays modest, because no source gives its size in 1206.

06

One palette

All frames share one grade, “Çini Turquoise”: cool turquoise in the shadows and mid-tones, while sunlit stone, copper, wood and skin stay warm. No purple, no pink.

The new colour was only used as a guide and transferred onto the original pixels, so the fine texture of stone and fabric stays untouched.

07

Frame to motion

Frames became video with Kling 3.0 Pro. Each shot starts on an approved frame and ends on the frame where the next shot begins, so the whole opening runs as one continuous camera. We picked the model after testing eleven video models on the same first and last frames in September 2026.

08

The star and the serpents

The faculty's emblem of two serpents comes from a carved panel on this gate, a motif Seljuk builders used as a talisman against illness. In the film a Seljuk star carved into the stone turns into the two serpents. The motion was drawn first as six poses, then animated.

Edit
